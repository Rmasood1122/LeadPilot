## 1. robots.txt (proposed explicit version)

Functionally the same as today (everything allowed). Naming each AI crawler documents the decision
and satisfies the brief's gate. Under the robots.txt standard (RFC 9309), a crawler that matches a
named group ignores the `*` group, so every named group repeats `Allow: /`. Do not add `Disallow`
lines for marketing pages.

## 2. sitemap.xml structure

Keep generating it from `site-pages.json`. Add GEO pages in the waves defined in
`technical/site_architecture.md`, never all at once. `priority` and `changefreq` are hints; Google
ignores them, and Bing uses `lastmod`. The real signals are: the page is in the sitemap, has an
accurate `lastmod`, is linked from navigation or footer, and is submitted via IndexNow on change.

Target `site-pages.json` entries after Wave 1 (existing 7 + 6 new):

| Path | priority | changefreq | Wave |
|---|---|---|---|
| / | 1.0 | weekly | live |
| /how-it-works/ | 0.9 | monthly | live |
| /pricing/ | 0.9 | monthly | live |
| /compare/leadpilot-vs-apollo/ | 0.8 | monthly | 1 |
| /compare/leadpilot-vs-instantly/ | 0.8 | monthly | 1 |

| /for/agencies/ | 0.8 | monthly | 1 |
| /for/b2b-service-firms/ | 0.8 | monthly | 1 |
| /for/local-businesses/ | 0.8 | monthly | 1 |
| /about/ | 0.6 | monthly | live |
| /contact/ | 0.6 | yearly | live |
| /privacy/, /terms/ | 0.3 | yearly | live |
| /faq/, /what-is-leadpilot/, /outbound-statistics/, /compare/ hub, /compare/leadpilot-vs-clay/, /compare/leadpilot-vs-artisan/ | 0.7–0.8 | monthly | 2 (needs founder approval to exceed the 6-page cap, Q-13) |

Example of the generated XML for one new entry:

```xml
<url>
  <loc>https://www.calendarharvest.com/compare/leadpilot-vs-apollo/</loc>
  <lastmod>2026-10-20</lastmod>
  <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
  <priority>0.8</priority>
</url>
```
(`lastmod` is whatever `frontend/scripts/seo/lastmod.mjs` writes from git, never typed by hand.)

After each deploy, run `node frontend/scripts/seo/indexnow_submit.mjs` (Bing, Yandex and other
IndexNow engines) and resubmit the sitemap in Google Search Console.

## 3. llms.txt (template, ready to publish at /llms.txt)

What to expect: llms.txt is a community proposal (llmstxt.org). No major AI provider has confirmed
reading it, and Google says Search ignores it. It's cheap and harmless, so publish it, but don't count
on it. SPEC 2.7 asks that it be **generated from `product-facts.json`** (the live facts) so it can't
drift. This is the target output. The capability lines must match the facts file at publish time.
Publish it only after the trademark review (Q-04).

```markdown

> LeadPilot by Calendarharvest is an AI outbound sales tool for agencies and B2B service firms: it researches your market, finds and verifies business contacts, writes one-to-one emails, sends on a schedule you approve each day, and books meetings.

LeadPilot is the approve-first AI outbound system for founder-led agencies and B2B service firms (5–50 people) who want booked sales meetings from a plain description of what they sell, without building lead lists, writing sequences or hiring an SDR. The customer describes a product or service; LeadPilot runs a 72-step research pipeline (144 steps when there are no past clients), checks the resulting strategy against 10 criteria including compliance, sources and email-verifies business contacts, writes each email for one person, and sends from the customer's own Gmail after the account owner approves the day's sending (10 emails on day one, rising to at most 100 a day per mailbox by default). Replies are classified and routed to the customer; LeadPilot does not reply to prospects. Prospects book on LeadPilot booking pages or a Calendly link.

Calendarharvest is a bootstrapped company; founder Rehan Masood (Astoria, New York). The site went live around September 2026. As of October 2026 there are no published customer results or public reviews. Pricing (USD): Starter $149, Growth $349, Scale $699, Enterprise $1,499 per month (flat per account), or Pay per meeting at $0 per month plus $179 per prospect who books.
```

<how_ai_assistants_choose_what_to_recommend>
Researched 2026-10-13. This explains why each task below exists.
1. The site went live around September 2026, after current models' training data was collected, so assistants can only name it through live web retrieval: ChatGPT search uses OAI-SearchBot; Claude uses Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User; Perplexity uses PerplexityBot; Meta AI uses Meta-WebIndexer; Google AI Overviews and AI Mode use Googlebot and the Google index, and Gemini Apps ground on the Google index (controlled by the Google-Extended token); Microsoft Copilot uses Bing (Bingbot), which supports IndexNow. A page these crawlers cannot fetch, or fetch without its text, cannot be cited.
2. Google's official guidance (updated July 2026): no special files, schema or "chunking" are needed for AI features, and Google Search ignores llms.txt. Mass-produced pages violate its spam policies, which since May 2026 also cover AI answers. Google says chasing inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem". Structured data is not required for AI features; Google uses it for rich results, and it must match the visible text.
3. The GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) found that adding cited sources, real statistics and quotations made a retrieved page more visible in AI answers (up to about 40%); keyword stuffing did not help.
4. 2026 citation studies (agency research, lower confidence) agree that for "best X software" questions, "best X" listicles — often written by competing vendors — are the most-cited page type; one small ChatGPT study (40 prompts, one snapshot) found vendor-owned pages, mostly self-ranking "best tools" posts, made up about half of citations. So this task makes the site eligible, accurate and citable; a separate human workstream earns third-party mentions.
5. No change to a website can guarantee a top-3 placement. Assistants and other sites decide that.
